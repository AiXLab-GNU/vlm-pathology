The six targets occupy different biological and clinical levels: Grade/ISUP, tumor
phenotype/content, PTEN loss, AR activity, SPOP mutation, and recurrence. The primary result
is their comparative evidence hierarchy; ISUP is highlighted later only because it reached an
additional functional-sensitivity experiment. Every numerical alignment result used an available
target-specific reference paired to the slide-derived embedding at the registered analysis unit.
Unequal reference coverage limited which external, conditional, or functional questions could
be asked; it was not converted into a negative association. Thus, not evaluated or blocked
denotes a missing prerequisite, whereas unsupported denotes an available comparison that did
not qualify the proposed signal. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-summary} summarizes the headline
states, and Supplementary Section S1 retains the detailed target, paired-reference,
availability, metric, and biological definitions.

The hierarchy is therefore not a ranking of which biological target is intrinsically most
visible in H\&E. It tests the paper's central claim that human-defined coordinates become useful
for reliability audit only to the extent supported by target-specific evidence: some can anchor
cross-cohort comparison, some require explanation to be withheld or qualified, and recoverability
alone does not establish use by a downstream decision.

\subsection{The study separates recoverability, reproducibility, and functional use}

We followed each target across the evidence axes permitted by its data: recoverability in a
frozen representation, unchanged-probe external transport, and clinical, site, representation,
sampling, or endpoint qualification (Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-map}). These are target-specific
evidence states, not model-confidence scores or clinical thresholds. For grade alone, a separate
internal analysis used locked BCR heads, target-specific erasure, and matched controls to test
functional sensitivity. Phenotype functional testing was blocked by unavailable same-cohort
independent truth; PTEN- and AR-directed functional-erasure experiments were feasible but not
run; SPOP lacked a qualified direction; and recurrence was the predicted outcome rather than an
input target for erasure. External functional transport was encoder-specific rather than
universal: Virchow passed CHIMERA, whereas CONCH did not, and neither encoder passed LEOPARD.

\paragraph{How to read the qualification map (Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-map}).}
Read rows from left to right. A supported cell applies only to the named axis and does not fill
missing evidence elsewhere. The functional-use column distinguishes a completed internal test
from an unavailable prerequisite, a feasible analysis not run, an unqualified direction, and a
non-applicable question; probe recoverability must not be mistaken for head use.

\subsection{Morphology-linked targets provide the best-qualified candidate coordinates}
\label{sec:results-morphology}

Grade and tumor phenotype were evaluated in NADT-Prostate and transferred without
target-cohort refitting to PANDA; PRECISE supplied a separate session-level grade estimate.
In NADT-Prostate, the patient-level CONCH grade probe had Spearman $\rho=0.478$
(patient-bootstrap interval 0.170--0.712; 39 patients), and tumor phenotype had $\rho=0.800$
(0.625--0.909; 39 patients). The locked grade probe retained positive associations in PANDA
Karolinska ($\rho=0.354$; 565 case images) and Radboud ($\rho=0.519$; 572 case images), while
the phenotype probe discriminated grade-derived tumor status with AUROCs of 0.786 and 0.871.
A separate PRECISE evaluation yielded grade $\rho=0.865$ across 17 imaging sessions
(Figure~\ref{fig:known-target-alignment}).

These results support directional transport, not uniform effect sizes or functional use.
PANDA phenotype used grade-derived tumor-versus-benign status rather than an independent
same-patient quantitative tumor-content reference; it therefore shows directional phenotype
transport, not exact external tumor-content agreement. Saved target-cohort intervals were
unavailable for PANDA and PRECISE and were not reconstructed. The complete cohort, unit,
denominator, metric, and uncertainty frame is in Supplementary Section S4.

The positive contribution of these experiments is that locked, source-trained probes retained
the direction of familiar morphology-linked information without target-cohort refitting. Grade
and phenotype can therefore serve as candidate clinician-interpretable coordinates for comparing
model behavior across compatible cohorts. They provide the strongest support in this study for
the claim that at least some human-defined quantities can anchor a contestable model audit; the
reference and uncertainty limitations prevent a stronger claim of interchangeable measurement.

\subsection{Molecular coordinates separate into recoverable, conditional, and unsupported states}
\label{sec:results-molecular}

The molecular frame comprised 273 TCGA-PRAD patients. PTEN loss was recoverable with a
frozen-primary CONCH AUROC of 0.632 (0.560--0.706), and AR activity had a positive Spearman
correlation of 0.195 (0.074--0.310). SPOP was near chance (AUROC 0.519, 0.408--0.627), and
its broader correlated seed-cell range crossed chance (0.348--0.679).

Conditional audits narrowed PTEN and AR (Figure~\ref{fig:conditional-alignment}). Held-out
increments beyond grade were $+0.019$ AUROC ($-0.025$ to $+0.068$) for CONCH PTEN and
$+0.035$ ($-0.013$ to $+0.087$) for Virchow PTEN. AR increments in $R^2$ were $+0.004$
($-0.036$ to $+0.042$) and $+0.019$ ($-0.015$ to $+0.051$), respectively. The pooled AR
direction was positive, but all six stored site-specific intervals crossed zero; evaluable SPOP
site intervals included or touched chance, and one site had no positive event.

Recoverable molecular information was therefore not shown to add held-out information beyond
grade or to transport across sites. Site remained entangled with acquisition and case mix.
The SPOP genomic reference was available and was used, so its unsupported state is an evaluated
lack of qualification in this frozen design, not a missing-label result. None of these findings
establishes molecular diagnosis from H\&E or use by a disease-prediction head.

The contrast among PTEN, AR, and SPOP is itself evidence for the proposed qualification
framework. PTEN and AR show that molecular covariation can be accessible to a frozen
representation yet fail to qualify as grade-independent or site-transported explanation. SPOP
shows that the framework can reject a plausible coordinate when the required genomic reference
is available but the signal does not separate reliably from chance. Together, these results
directly support separating information recovery from stable explanatory value, rather than
treating every decodable target as a trustworthy account of model behavior.

\subsection{Outcome alignment changes with endpoint and clinical reference}
\label{sec:results-outcome}

A post-hoc recurrence score fitted in LEOPARD was applied unchanged to TCGA-PRAD. In the
270-patient transfer frame, reconstructed with-tumor recurrence yielded a C-index of 0.673
(0.587--0.759; 57 events), whereas official TCGA-CDR PFI yielded 0.586 (0.482--0.689;
42 events). On 153 complete cases, adding the image score to grade changed C-index by
$+0.083$ ($+0.012$ to $+0.157$; 30 reconstructed events) but had an integrated-Brier-score
interval including zero. For official PFI, the C-index increment was $+0.032$
($-0.040$ to $+0.126$; 15 events), and all paired C-index and integrated-Brier-score intervals
included zero. Fuller clinical and site comparisons did not establish robust independent
increment.

Recurrence is therefore endpoint- and reference-conditioned, not a validated prognostic
biomarker. Concordance measures risk ordering rather than calibrated individual risk or
clinical usefulness, and the exploratory PCA sweep was not an unbiased model-selection
procedure. Supplementary Section S9 retains the endpoint definitions, complete paired
comparison family, and outcome-contrast figure.

The change in apparent value when the event definition and clinical comparator changed supports
the central claim from the outcome side: alignment is not an intrinsic property of an image score
alone, but of the score--endpoint--reference combination. A favorable comparison under one
reconstructed endpoint cannot therefore be promoted to a stable human--AI coordinate or reused
as evidence for prognosis under a non-equivalent endpoint.

\subsection{Representation audits bound sensitivity without selecting a universal model}
\label{sec:results-sensitivity}

Six targets were evaluated across 12 encoder--scale--tile configurations and five sampling
seeds, yielding 360 correlated seed cells and 390 seed-matched contrasts. Grade, phenotype,
PTEN, and AR retained their direction across observed configuration ranges; null-straddling
occurred in 8 of 12 for SPOP and 1 of 12 for recurrence
(Figure~\ref{fig:stability}). Because these repeats reused patients, folds, and representations,
they assess analytic sensitivity rather than independent replication or a universal
CONCH-versus-Virchow ranking. Supplementary Section S8 contains the complete grid and the
seed-matched encoder, physical-scale, and tile-budget contrast figure.

Directional persistence across settings makes the grade, phenotype, PTEN, and AR findings less
compatible with a single chosen configuration artifact, whereas frequent null crossing reinforces
the lack of a qualified SPOP coordinate and the conditional status of recurrence. This supports
the robustness layer of the evidence map, but not population generalization: no new patients,
institutions, or independent representations were introduced by the setting grid.

\subsection{ISUP shows encoder-specific external whole-tissue functional transport}
\label{sec:results-functional-sensitivity}

Grade/ISUP was the only axis for which the current data and protocol supported a locked-head
extension. The exploratory frame comprised 392 TCGA-PRAD patients with 80 BCR events. CONCH
and Virchow remained frozen; patient-disjoint ridge probes learned ISUP-correlated directions,
and ridge-penalized Cox heads learned BCR risk. The heads were then held fixed while the
ISUP-correlated direction was removed, with variance-matched random directions as controls.

ISUP was recoverable out of fold (Spearman $\rho=0.615$ for CONCH and $0.658$ for Virchow),
and the full BCR heads had C-indices of 0.627 (0.553--0.694) and 0.632 (0.564--0.691).
Under the registered positive-means-degradation convention, targeted removal produced
concordance losses of $+0.041$ (0.011--0.073) and $+0.023$ (0.007--0.040), respectively,
exceeding matched-random controls ($p=0.0099$ for each).
After refitting, differences narrowed to $+0.014$ ($-0.006$--0.035) and $+0.010$
($-0.003$--0.022). Limited ISUP-plus-AI versus ISUP-only differences were $-0.000$
($-0.039$--0.039) and $-0.032$ ($-0.088$--0.025). Intervals including zero indicate
potential compensation and do not establish indispensable use or absence of clinical increment.

The fixed-head result is the study's only direct bridge from representation availability to a
downstream judgment. Refit and ISUP-only results left compensation and clinical increment
unresolved, so it shows subspace sensitivity rather than a necessary human-equivalent mechanism.

A protocol-locked clean rerun preserved every estimate and gate; Supplementary Section S5A
reports the 20/20 hash audit. This is computational reproducibility, not an independent-cohort
replication, and failed detector sensitivity keeps the intervention whole-tissue.

Across seven held-out TCGA sites (289 patients; 69 events), CONCH yielded full-head C-index
0.612 and $\Delta_{use}=+0.048$, but its delta interval crossed zero. Virchow yielded 0.588 and
$\Delta_{use}=+0.033$ and passed. The family therefore provided encoder-specific rather than
replicated site-heldout evidence.

The locked LEOPARD reanalysis included 508 patients and 87 events. CONCH full-head C-index
was 0.517 (0.449--0.590) and targeted
$\Delta_{use}=+0.038$ (0.014--0.066); although erasure exceeded matched-random controls, the
head interval included 0.5. Virchow yielded a
full-head C-index of 0.457 (0.386--0.528) and $\Delta_{use}=+0.002$
($-0.008$--0.011). Neither encoder passed: the TCGA-derived direction was not shown to
transport with a valid LEOPARD BCR head.
Supplementary Section S5B reports the complete gate and matched-control results.

The preregistered CHIMERA analysis then evaluated all 95 CHIMERA patients (27 BCR events;
190 slides) without target-cohort fitting. The TCGA-locked ISUP direction was recoverable for
both encoders: Spearman $\rho=0.272$ (0.091--0.441) for CONCH and $0.392$
(0.189--0.555) for Virchow. Both full heads had point C-index 0.630. For CONCH, the interval
was 0.500--0.758 and therefore did not satisfy the strict lower-bound-above-chance gate.
Targeted erasure nevertheless reduced C-index by $+0.055$ (0.009--0.111), exceeding the
matched-random p95 of 0.045 (Holm $p=0.0198$). CONCH therefore remained fail or inconclusive
because a positive erasure effect could not rescue an unqualified full head.

Virchow's full-head C-index was 0.630 (0.512--0.753), and targeted erasure reduced C-index by
$+0.025$ (0.005--0.047), exceeding the matched-random p95 of 0.009
(Holm $p=0.0198$). Virchow passed every prespecified gate and qualified as external
whole-tissue functional transport. A clean analysis rerun reproduced all six nonvolatile
output hashes exactly. Because CHIMERA contained only 27 events, used tissue rather than tumor
masks, and did not establish endpoint equivalence, this result is model-specific external
sensitivity rather than a tumor mechanism, clinical validation, or a formal CONCH--Virchow
superiority comparison.

Grade therefore has internal functional sensitivity and encoder-specific external
whole-tissue transport, not universal transport across models or cohorts. It is not
indispensable, mechanistic, tumor-specific, or clinically incremental use. No unknown direction
was evaluated as a new marker.

\subsection{The final map distinguishes supported, conditional, and unsuitable coordinates}

The combined hierarchy retains unlike metrics and missing evidence rather than averaging them.
Grade and phenotype are the best-qualified representation coordinates; PTEN and AR are
conditional; SPOP is unsuitable in the frozen design; and recurrence is endpoint-sensitive.
Grade alone additionally has internal functional-sensitivity evidence and encoder-specific
external whole-tissue transport: Virchow passed CHIMERA, CONCH remained inconclusive, and
neither passed LEOPARD
(Figure~\ref{fig:human-ai-linkage}).

Taken together, the experiments support selective rather than universal interpretability. They
show that a foundation-model representation can expose several clinically recognizable quantities,
but that those quantities occupy different evidential stages and cannot all be treated as stable
explanations of a downstream decision. The contribution is the tested separation of transported,
conditional, unsupported, and functionally probed coordinates---not a claim that one metric or
one probe explains the model as a whole.

Figure~\ref{fig:human-ai-linkage} links each clinical coordinate to its operational probe-defined
feature and the available evidence about downstream judgment. This does not mean that only ISUP
describes prostate cancer: phenotype is strongly represented, PTEN- and AR-directed functional
tests are feasible follow-up experiments that were not run, SPOP did not yield a qualified
representation feature, and recurrence
requires external prognostic validation. What is unique to ISUP is evidence that the tested BCR
heads were sensitive to its correlated embedding direction.
