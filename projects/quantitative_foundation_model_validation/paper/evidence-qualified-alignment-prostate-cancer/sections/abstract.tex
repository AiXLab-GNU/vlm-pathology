\begin{abstract}
Prostate-cancer AI can distinguish states, but clinicians need to know which human-interpretable
quantitative axes its representations encode, whether those axes survive cohort and technical
variation, and whether downstream decisions use them. We audited six non-interchangeable axes
in frozen CONCH and Virchow representations: Grade/ISUP, tumor phenotype/content, PTEN loss, AR
activity, SPOP mutation, and recurrence. Among the six axes, ISUP alone underwent locked-head
functional tests. Grade and phenotype showed the strongest representation evidence and
transport; PTEN and AR were conditional, SPOP was unsupported, and recurrence changed with
endpoint and reference. Internal targeted
erasure reduced two biochemical-recurrence heads' concordance beyond matched-random controls.
In seven-site-held-out TCGA, only Virchow met the prespecified functional gate. In independent-
patient LEOPARD (508 patients; 87 events), neither TCGA-trained head showed discrimination with
a confidence interval excluding chance, so external functional transport was not qualified.
This comparative map provides an actionable audit vocabulary: transported coordinates can
anchor clinician review of agreement and disagreement, conditional or unsupported axes indicate
where explanations should be withheld, and gaps prioritize external or functional validation.
It enables clinician-understandable reliability audits and bounds future residual-signal
discovery; it does not establish clinical benefit, strong functional use, or new biomarkers.
\end{abstract}
